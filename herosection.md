What I'd Add

There are three sections I'd strongly recommend adding because they build trust without requiring a large client portfolio.

1. Engineering Principles

Instead of saying you're "professional," demonstrate how you work.

Security by Design
Scalable Architecture
Clean, Maintainable Code
Automated Testing
Documentation
Performance First

These are subtle signals that experienced technical buyers notice.

2. Engagement Models

Clients often wonder "How do we work together?"

Present three clear options:

End-to-End Product Development
Dedicated Engineering Team
Technical Consulting & Architecture

This helps prospects quickly see how Apeiroq can fit their needs.

3. FAQ

Answer common buying questions before they're asked.

How long does a project typically take?
Can you work with our existing team?
Do you take over existing codebases?
How do you estimate project cost?
What happens after launch?
Do you sign NDAs?
Can you work across time zones?
Overall positioning

The homepage shouldn't feel like a freelancer's portfolio or a traditional software agency. It should feel like the website of a product engineering partner—a team that combines strategic thinking, technical depth, and long-term collaboration.

The message a visitor should leave with is:

"Apeiroq understands how to take a software product from idea to production, and I'd be working directly with the engineers who build it."

That positioning is stronger, more differentiated, and scales well as your case study portfolio grows.
